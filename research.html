01 Research

Research

Planets do not begin with atmospheres and orbits already set. They grow inside disks, interact with other bodies, migrate, lose material, and build or transform their atmospheres. By studying several stages of that history, I want to understand which paths can produce a terrestrial planet capable of supporting life.

I investigate coupled signatures of planet formation and evolution across natal-disk volatile chemistry, spin-orbit architecture, and transmission spectroscopy using JWST/NIRISS and MIRI data, atmospheric retrievals, radial-velocity time series, astrochemical models, and population-level inference.

01 / Primary focus

Exoplanet atmospheres & retrievals

I use JWST observations to read the chemical fingerprints in exoplanet atmospheres and learn about clouds, water, and the conditions on distant worlds.

I retrieve molecular abundances and aerosol properties from JWST transmission spectra, then combine archival samples with machine learning and hierarchical population analysis to test cloud trends across super-Earths and sub-Neptunes.

02 / System architecture

Exoplanet dynamics

A planet’s orbit preserves clues about its past. I use those clues to work out how common planetary systems moved and changed.

I model Rossiter-McLaughlin radial-velocity time series to infer the sky-projected obliquity, λ, and place spin-orbit architecture in the context of multiplicity, secular excitation, scattering, and disk-driven migration.

03 / Planet formation

Protoplanetary disk chemistry

Before planets exist, gas and dust orbit young stars. I trace water, carbon, and organic molecules in the warm inner regions where rocky planets begin.

I interpret JWST/MIRI molecular emission and absorption with astrochemical models to constrain inner-disk water, hydrocarbon networks, carbon-grain destruction, and the gas-phase C/O ratio inherited during rocky-planet assembly.
